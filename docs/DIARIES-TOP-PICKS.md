# Diaries of the Greats — Best Picks

> Diaries that are public domain, fascinating, and fit OddHobb's audience.

---

## Top 5 Diaries to Start With

### 1. Samuel Pepys (1633-1703)
**Why:** The most vivid diary in English history. He wrote about the Great Fire of London, the plague, his love life, the theater, politics, daily life in 1660s London. It reads like a Netflix series.

**Best entries:**
- The Great Fire of London (September 1666)
- The plague year (1665)
- His affair with Deb Willett
- The coronation of Charles II
- Daily life — meals, friends, work, guilt

**Word count:** ~1.25 million words over 9 years
**Episodes possible:** 20+ (each entry is a story)
**Audience appeal:** History buffs, literary types, curious minds

### 2. Leonardo da Vinci (1452-1519)
**Why:** The notebooks are a window into the greatest mind in history. Art, science, invention, anatomy, engineering — all in his mirror-writing. He designed helicopters, studied human anatomy, painted the Mona Lisa, and wrote about everything.

**Best entries:**
- Anatomical drawings and observations
- Invention designs (flying machines, tanks)
- Paintings in progress (Mona Lisa, Last Supper)
- Personal notes and to-do lists
- Observations about nature and light

**Word count:** ~7,200 pages across notebooks
**Episodes possible:** 50+ (endless)
**Audience appeal:** Nerds, artists, inventors, curious minds

### 3. Charles Darwin (1809-1882)
**Why:** The voyage of the Beagle diary is one of the greatest travel narratives ever written. A young man on a five-year voyage, collecting specimens, making observations, and slowly realizing that everything he believed about creation was wrong.

**Best entries:**
- The Galápagos Islands
- Encounters with indigenous peoples
- The earthquake in Chile
- Collecting beetles and finches
- The slow realization about evolution

**Word count:** ~1,500 pages across diaries
**Episodes possible:** 20+
**Audience appeal:** Science nerds, nature lovers, curious minds

### 4. Virginia Woolf (1882-1941)
**Why:** One of the greatest writers in English, her diaries are intimate, honest, and beautiful. She writes about writing, mental health, relationships, war, and the passage of time. It's like eavesdropping on a genius.

**Best entries:**
- Writing the waves
- Her husband Leonard
- Mental breakdowns
- World War II
- Daily observations about light, weather, people

**Word count:** ~5,000 pages across diaries
**Episodes possible:** 30+
**Audience appeal:** Writers, literary types, people who think deeply

### 5. Frida Kahlo (1907-1954)
**Why:** Her diary is raw, painful, and beautiful. Paintings, drawings, words about pain, love, politics, and art. She wrote about her tumultuous marriage to Diego Rivera, her political activism, and her physical suffering.

**Best entries:**
- Paintings in the diary
- Letters to Diego
- Political commentary
- Observations about Mexican culture
- Her pain and resilience

**Word count:** ~250 pages (diary + letters)
**Episodes possible:** 15+
**Audience appeal:** Artists, political types, people who understand pain
