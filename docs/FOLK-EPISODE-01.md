Every culture has a story about the wind. In Norse mythology, the North Wind isn't just weather — he's a person. A god with moods, gifts, and grudges. And tonight, I'm going to tell you the story of the man who asked the North Wind for a gift, and got more than he bargained for.

**[STORY — 0:30-18:00]**

### Part 1: The Poor Miller (0:30-3:00)

Once there was a miller who had a son named Halvar. The miller was poor — not desperately poor, but poor enough to worry. His mill was old, the river was slow, and the grain didn't always come.

Halvar was a good son. He worked the mill, he mended the wheels, he carried the grain. But he was young, and he dreamed of more. He wanted to travel. He wanted to see the world beyond the valley.

One evening, as the sun was setting behind the mountains, Halvar climbed to the top of the hill behind the mill. He stood there, looking out at the horizon, and he spoke to the sky.

"I wish I could travel," he said. "I wish I could see the world."

The wind answered. Not with words — wind never speaks in words. But with a sound. A long, low whistle that came from the north, from the mountains, from the place where the North Wind lived.

### Part 2: The North Wind's Cave (3:00-7:00)

The next morning, Halvar walked north. He walked for three days, through forests and over mountains, until he came to a cave in the side of a cliff. The cave was dark and cold, and the wind howled at its entrance.

Inside, the North Wind was waiting. He was old — older than the mountains, older than the trees. His beard was white as snow, his eyes were gray as storm clouds, and his voice was the sound of wind through bare branches.

"You called me," the North Wind said.

"I did," Halvar replied. "I wish to travel. I wish to see the world."

The North Wind laughed — a sound like autumn leaves skittering across stone. "Everyone wants to travel," he said. "But few understand what they're asking for."

"I understand," Halvar said. "I want adventure. I want to see things I've never seen."

"Very well," the North Wind said. "I will give you a gift. But know this: every gift has a price. And the price is not always what you expect."

The North Wind blew — a single, sharp breath — and Halvar was gone.

### Part 7: The Lesson (18:00-20:00)

Halvar returned home. He was older now — not in years, but in understanding. He had seen the world. He had met kings and beggars, scholars and thieves. He had learned that adventure was not about the destination — it was about what you carried with you.

He returned to the mill. He worked the wheels. He carried the grain. But he was different now. He was at peace.

And when the wind blew from the north, he would look up and smile. Because he knew that the North Wind was watching. And the North Wind was pleased.
