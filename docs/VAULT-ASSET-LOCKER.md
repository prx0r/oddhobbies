# OddHobb Vault — The Asset Locker Concept

> Buy the object once → receive the digital asset → keep it in your Vault → reuse on future products.
> Saved verbatim from research.

---

Yes. That is the much stronger version of the business.

The Etsy product is no longer just **"here is a £16.99 object."** It is:

> **Buy the OddHobb object once → also receive the underlying digital asset → keep it in your OddHobb Vault → use that same asset for future compatible products.**

That gives us a reason for customers to create an OddHobb account without discount-spamming them or trying to drag the Etsy transaction elsewhere.

One important wording change: I would **not** promise "permanent ownership on OddHobb.com." Better:

> **Includes your downloadable 3D asset and a perpetual personal-use licence, plus a copy saved to your OddHobb Vault.**

They should actually receive/download the mesh, not merely have access dependent on our website continuing forever. That makes the promise much stronger and cleaner.

For example, imagine the custom cribbage product:

**£14.99 — Custom Cribbage Character Peg Set**

Included:
- 6 physical pegs
- your custom character/head mesh
- STL download
- GLB/OBJ preview asset
- perpetual personal-use licence
- saved in **My OddHobb**
- reuse the same character on future OddHobb-compatible products

Then suddenly next Christmas:

> "You already own Dad's Fisherman character. Put him on a chess king, game-night trophy or golf-ball marker."

That is where this gets powerful.

### Etsy needs to remain the completed transaction

Current Etsy policy is the bit we need to respect. Etsy explicitly says sellers must not facilitate off-platform transactions, and its current communication rules prohibit using links/QR codes **for the purpose of moving the purchase elsewhere**.

So the structure should be:

**GOOD**

> "Your Etsy purchase includes the physical product + downloadable asset. You may optionally claim and manage your included asset in your OddHobb Vault."

**BAD**

> "It's £19.99 here, but visit oddhobb.com and buy it for £14.99."

We should never make Etsy merely an advertisement for an off-platform checkout.

Etsy also explicitly permits seller-designed digital products and third-party production partners, provided those are correctly disclosed. Seller-prompted AI creations must currently disclose use of AI.

I'd also ensure the **actual promised file is delivered through Etsy** wherever possible. Etsy supports instant and made-to-order digital downloads.

So the claim experience becomes an **additional convenience**, not something required to receive what they bought.

### And yes: price aggressively fairly

I think this is key.

Don't play the usual:

> £1.20 plastic → £39.99 because "personalized".

Instead:

**£9–15** digital projects
**£12–20** tiny personalized objects
**£20–35** substantial hobby accessories
**£30–60** kits
**£10–18** CDs
**£15–30** collector displays.

Then add **way more value than expected**.

Someone spends £17.99 and gets:

> beautiful physical object + underlying 3D file + permanent licence + online 3D viewer + reuseability.

That creates the reaction we want:

> "Why is this company giving me all this?"

And because our production stack is automated, the answer is: **because it costs us almost nothing to give them the digital layer once we've already generated it.**

### This also solves a major ecommerce problem

Most Etsy shops have essentially zero durable customer relationship.

They sell:

**item → transaction ends.**

OddHobb becomes:

**item → asset → account → collection → compatible products → tutorials → next hobby object.**

The physical object is the acquisition mechanism.

The **asset library is the retention mechanism**.

And the Gold Probe/content system continuously supplies the next thing the existing customers could use.

I think the website positioning could eventually be incredibly simple:

> **ODDHOBB**
> *Stuff for people who are really into stuff.*

Then underneath:

> **Every custom OddHobb creation is yours to keep — physical and digital.**

That feels much more substantial than "another Etsy 3D-print shop."
