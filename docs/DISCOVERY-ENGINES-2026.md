# OddHobb — Discovery Engines & 2026 Trend Report

> Three discovery engines: occupation identity, hobby identity, Gen-Z identity hobby.
> Saved verbatim from research. Pinterest August 2026 hobby report = OddHobb roadmap.

---

There are really **three discovery engines** here, and they can feed the same OddHobb + sleepy-content machine:

**1. occupation identity** — electrician, mechanic, carpenter, watchmaker, machinist, luthier  
**2. hobby identity** — knitting, needlepoint, mahjong, miniature painting, fountain pens  
**3. emerging Gen-Z identity hobby** — zines, cyberdecks, goth pottery, miniatures, journaling, visible mending

I would **not market explicitly around "neurodivergent people"**. The occupational data is messy and people vary enormously. One large 2024 autistic-employment study found healthcare/welfare, IT, and public/army/charity were the three most common sectors in its Dutch sample, while another recent sample had transportation/material-moving, office support and food-service near the top. There isn't a clean universal "ADHD people are electricians" dataset.

But as an **internal discovery heuristic**, it is useful: look for communities with intense interests, tactile/repetitive workflows, collecting, specialist gear, and strong identity. Current ADHD threads also contain a lot of trades/mechanical work anecdotally, but that's community evidence, not prevalence data.

## Tradespeople are another excellent gift market — but don't sell them tools

Current Reddit gift threads reveal the fundamental issue.

People repeatedly ask what to buy an electrician/carpenter/plumber who **already owns the basic tools**, including a post literally yesterday asking for something unusual/luxurious an experienced electrician wouldn't buy himself.

And tradespeople themselves point out that tools are hard gifts because professionals are particular about brands/styles.

That's an opportunity.

Don't compete with Knipex or Milwaukee.

Sell the **culture around the trade**.

So I'd now think of OddHobb as targeting two overlapping waves:

**OLD HOBBY → NEW YOUNG AUDIENCE**  
mahjong, needlepoint, knitting, embroidery, pottery, bookbinding, whittling.

**DIGITAL NATIVE → PHYSICAL HOBBY**  
cyberdecks, mechanical keyboards, zines, journaling, miniatures, K-pop photocards, retro gaming.

Both groups want **little objects that make the hobby feel more theirs**.

If I were allocating the next product/content probes right now, I'd go hardest on **Needlepoint, Mahjong, Cyberdecks, Journaling/Zines, Miniatures, Sashiko/visible mending, Goth Pottery, Model Cars, Home Barista and Watchmaking**. Those give you a very strong mixture of live trend momentum, wealthy enthusiast markets, cheap 3D-printable accessories, sleepy-content potential, and giftability.
